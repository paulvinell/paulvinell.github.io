**The curse of tools**


I feel like integration of "knowledge" or insights achieved – say during meditation – do not translate into changes to my everyday state. They may in rare cases do so indirectly, as deliberate applications of knowledge and voluntary changes in behavior can create new experiences and from there transform into intuition.

I think we also have to think about what we are optimizing. The totality of what your life is, is composed of many different substates and contexts. Your insights, e.g. a relational one, will only apply in some amount of these scenarios. And it has to be applied voluntarily, i.e. with effort.

Somewhat inherently in the charm of tools is the idea that if we gather enough of them, we can cover all our "problem areas" and just feel good. In reality, you will at any moment in time overthink which tool to apply. The more mental tools you have, the more cognitively demanding it will be to apply them. It also reinforces the idea that you need tools. Suddenly you need tools for every unpleasant thing in your life, and your mind will create problems surrounding your tooling. Thoughts like "It's not working well enough" or "I will feel bad if I don't use this."

In fact, any tool you use is prone to habituation; that is, its effectiveness lowers with repeated use. Do you fight it out – look for new tools or ways to reinvigorate your now blunted strategies?

How sure are you that your need for tools is not just a way to avoid what you deem too unpleasant to face? Maybe you should be maximizing that discomfort, not minimizing it. Are you sure that while they provide utility on their own, they are not in their totality detracting from your experience of life?
